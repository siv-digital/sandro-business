The playbook's Grow / Transition / Manage labels stay what they are: the pathway outcome of the assessment. The tracks are the plain-language version
of the same sorting, said the way Alok said it in the meeting.
The Committed Seller 	Track: Ready to Sell 	Selling in 12 to 18 months, or already in a deal
"I've decided. Now I need to not screw it up."
W H O T H I S I S
An owner in their late 50s to 60s who has already made
the emotional decision to sell, or had it made for them by
an unsolicited offer. The business is the bulk of their net
worth. They may already be fielding calls from a buyer or
a private equity firm, which means the clock started
without them.
W H AT J U S T H A P P E N E D
A buyer or PE firm called with a number that got their
attention
A health scare, theirs or a partner's
A competitor sold and the multiple made the rounds
A partner wants out and the buy-sell can't fund it
W H AT T H E Y WA N T
Certainty. What the business is actually worth, what
they'll keep after taxes, and a deal that closes without
falling apart or leaking to employees and customers.
W H AT T H E Y ' R E A F R A I D O F
Leaving money on the table. Taxes taking a bite that
planning could have prevented. Being outmatched in
diligence by a buyer who does this for a living. And
quietly: what their life looks like the day after the wire hits.
W H AT T H E Y N E E D T O H E A R
It's not too late to plan, but the sequence starts now. The
estate and tax structures that matter only count if they're
in place well before the sale. One team should
quarterback the whole thing, and the sale is the middle of
the process, not the end of it.
W H E R E T H E Y S H O W U P
They answer cold email now because the timing got
personal. They register for webinars with transaction
topics in the title. They come in referred by a CPA or
attorney who heard "I got an offer." They search for
valuation terms at 11pm.
T H E I R J O U R N E Y
1. Assessment scores 8 to 10. Manage pathway. Deal created, fast track.
2. Discovery call confirms the timeline and surfaces the deal status (offer in hand vs. going to market).
3. Valuation plus personal financial plan and gap analysis run in quick succession. Usually little or no gap; the work
is confirming the number, not growing it.
4. Pre-sale planning starts immediately: estate structures with the estate planning partner, wealth and tax plan
with Sandro, run together. The IRS respects structures set up well ahead of a sale, not ones bolted on the month
before close.
5. The sale runs through the M&A partner: buyer outreach, indications of interest, LOI, diligence, close. Sandro stays
at the table as quarterback.
6. At close, Sandro activates the wealth plan and manages the proceeds. This persona becomes the core Sandro
client.
Internal: this is the persona the whole platform exists to find. Every marketing dollar ultimately prices against how many of
these we reach before the buyer's banker does.
The Value Builder 	Track: Build Value First 	Three to five years out. The number isn't there yet.
"I want to sell. I just can't afford to yet."
W H O T H I S I S
An owner in their late 40s to 50s who wants out on a
three-to-five-year horizon and is willing to wait, because
the first pass at the numbers didn't work. The valuation
came in under the wealth goal, or the business depends
on them so heavily a buyer would discount it hard.
W H AT J U S T H A P P E N E D
They did the math on a napkin and it didn't cover the
life they want
Our valuation and gap analysis made the shortfall
specific
A banker or buyer passed, or offered a number that
stung
A peer told them what actually drives multiples
W H AT T H E Y WA N T
A bigger number at exit, a business that runs without
them, and a plan with an end date. They want to know the
years of work will actually move the valuation, not just
keep a consultant busy.
W H AT T H E Y ' R E A F R A I D O F
Value enhancement that never ends. Owners in these
programs have a habit of never selling; they just keep
enhancing. This persona has heard those stories, and the
fear is spending three years and six figures to end up
where they started.
W H AT T H E Y N E E D T O H E A R
The gap is a number, so the work has a finish line. The
value growth advisor works the specific gaps the
valuation surfaced. Sandro stays at the table every
quarter, and when the value is there, the sale track is
already built and waiting.
W H E R E T H E Y S H O W U P
Webinar regulars. They download the playbook and
actually read it. They sit in nurture for months, open most
emails, and book the call after a quarterly earnings scare
or a birthday that ends in zero.
T H E I R J O U R N E Y
1. Assessment scores 0 to 7. Transition pathway (or Grow at the long end). Deal created and held open.
2. Discovery call and valuation confirm the gap; the personal financial plan makes it concrete.
3. Handoff to a value growth advisor. The advisor does the inside-the-business work; Sandro does not.
4. Quarterly touchpoints, owned by Sandro, on the calendar from day one. Progress is measured against the
number that set the track.
5. When the value closes the gap, the business is re-valued and the owner moves onto the Ready to Sell track.
Internal: this is the track where relationships die if we let them. The handoff must come with a referral fee agreement and
calendar-committed quarterly reviews, or the value growth firm inherits the client. The touchpoint cadence is the retention
mechanism, not a courtesy.
The Quiet Explorer 	Track: Still Exploring 	No timeline. Gathering information.
"I'm not selling. I'm just... looking."
W H O T H I S I S
An owner anywhere from 40 to 65 with no timeline and
no commitment. They found the website, sat silently in a
webinar, or downloaded the playbook under a personal
email address. They might sell in five years. They might
die at their desk. They don't know yet, and they don't want
to be asked.
W H AT J U S T H A P P E N E D
A friend sold their company and something shifted
They realized their kids don't want the business
A podcast or article put a number in their head
Nothing happened, and that's starting to worry them
W H AT T H E Y WA N T
To learn without being pursued. A rough sense of what
the business might be worth, what a process looks like,
and what people like them regret. Anonymity while they
figure out what they think.
W H AT T H E Y ' R E A F R A I D O F
Being sold to. Tipping their hand to anyone, including their
own family. Looking naive in a conversation with people
who do deals every week. So they stay quiet and read.
W H AT T H E Y N E E D T O H E A R
Take your time. Here is genuinely useful material, no
meeting required. When the timeline becomes real, the
first step is a short survey, and it will still be here.
W H E R E T H E Y S H O W U P
Organic social and search, including AI answers. Webinar
registrations that never turn into questions. The monthly
owner list. Eventually the book. This is the audience the
10,000-member community is built from.
T H E I R J O U R N E Y
1. Enters through content: a webinar, the playbook, a post, a search result.
2. Joins the owner list. Monthly follow-up, useful every time, never pushy.
3. Lead scoring watches engagement: opens, downloads, webinar attendance, key page visits.
4. No deal is created and no one calls them. The assessment is always one click away.
5. When they take it, they convert into one of the other two personas and the journey starts for real.
Internal: this bucket is measured in list growth and engagement, not meetings booked. It funds the pipeline two years from
now. Treating explorers like sellers is how you lose both.
