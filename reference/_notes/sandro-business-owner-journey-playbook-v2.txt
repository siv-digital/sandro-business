I P H A S E I · D I S C O V E R Y & D I A G N O S T I C S
Know where you stand.
H O W P H A S E I R U N S 01 The assessment survey, taken online in about ten minutes. 02 A structured discovery call. 03 A market-based
valuation of the business.
Up to 80% of a mid-market owner's net worth sits inside the operating business, one illiquid, undiversified asset
(EPI
owner-readiness research; [VERIFY/cite final source]). Phase I is three steps, in order. Most owners have never done all three.
S T A R T H E R E
The assessment survey
A short survey on the business, your timeline, and what you want from both. It takes about ten minutes, there is
nothing to tally, and it places you on a track before anyone gets on a call.
The questions cover the ground buyers diligence: how much of the business runs without you, how your revenue is
built, how clean the financials are, what is already in place on the estate and tax side, and what you want the years
after the business to look like.

The business number meets the personal number. Phase I put a value on the
business; Phase II puts the second number next to it: what the rest of your life costs
to fund. Until both are real, every exit conversation is guesswork.
The personal financial plan · what does life after the business cost?
04 Personal financial
plan
What life after the business costs, stated as a number. One integrated plan: business value,
personal assets, retirement income needs, and your exit timeline.
Cash flow &

Projections and retirement-income modeling, stress-tested with Monte Carlo analysis for
confidence in the plan.
Estate & legacy 	Estate structure and beneficiary review, tax-efficient transfer strategies, post-sale investment
policy, charitable and legacy planning.
The wealth gap · your valuation against your number
05 Wealth gap analysis Your valuation against your number, after taxes and debt. If there is a gap, you see exactly how
wide it is, across everything you own. It is the central motivating force behind everything that
follows.
O N LY I F T H E G A P C A L L S F O R I T
Value enhancement
Focused 90-day sprints close the specific gaps the valuation surfaced, inside the business. The value is measured again as you go.
When the number is there, the path continues.
Where the gaps usually hide: owner dependency, the relationships and judgment that live only in your head; revenue quality, since
recurring contracted income carries a higher multiple than lumpy project work of the same size; and team retention, because the
management team is part of what is being bought.
W H A T P H A S E I I P R O D U C E S Two numbers you can act on, and the size of the distance between them. Clear your number at
today's value and you have earned
freedom of timing. Fall short, and the gap becomes a quantified
target with work attached to it.

III P H A S E I I I · P R E - S A L E P L A N N I N G
Put your plans in place.
Done before a buyer ever appears. The structures that decide what you keep only
work if they exist before the deal, and the window for setting them closes the
moment a number lands on the company.
06 Estate plan 	Trust and tax structures that only work if they exist before the deal. Beneficiaries, titling, and
transfer strategy reviewed while there is still time to act.
07 Wealth plan 	The plan for the proceeds, built before there are proceeds. Post-sale income, cash flow, and
drawdown strategy, ready the day the deal closes.
S E T B E F O R E T H E L E T T E R O F I N T E N T
The tax result is mostly written early. Qualified Small Business Stock (§1202) can exclude 50/75/100% of federal
capital gains at three/four/five years held, capped at the greater of $15 million or 10× basis, but the qualifying
decisions are made years out, or not at all. And vehicles such as GRATs and SLATs move the business interest, and
the appreciation still ahead of it, out of your taxable estate only
while the value is still low. Once a letter of intent
puts a number on the company, that window narrows sharply.
T H E T I M I N G P R I N C I P L E
Value-freezing and gain-exclusion strategies are won on the calendar. Set up before
the letter of intent, they can reshape your after-tax outcome; set up after, most of
the leverage is already gone.
W H A T P H A S E I I I P R O D U C E S An estate plan and a wealth plan that are already in force when the sale starts, so the deal is
executed into a structure rather than around one.

IV P H A S E I V · S A L E & T R A N S I T I O N
Sell the business.
Four milestones, one confidential process, on your timeline. Two owners can sell
identical businesses for the same price and keep wildly different amounts. The
difference is structure, whether the path is a sale, a succession, an ESOP, or a
recapitalization, and most of it is decided before the deal is signed.
The four milestones · one confidential process
08 CIM 	The confidential information memorandum: your story and your numbers, prepared for serious
buyers. Built with the team that runs your sale, with confidentiality agreements protecting the
process end to end.
09 IOI 	Indications of interest: buyers put their first numbers on the table. Weak offers and poor fits get
weeded out before they cost you time or leverage.
10 LOI 	A letter of intent with the best buyer, negotiated under exclusivity. The structure is reviewed for
the after-tax outcome, not just the headline price.
11 Closing 	Diligence runs, the purchase agreement is signed, and funds transfer. The wealth plan from
Phase III goes into effect for Phase V.
O U T C O M E The business is sold or transitioned, with a fully activated, tax-efficient, post-sale wealth plan in place. Phase V begins
immediately.
O N E P R O C E S S , N O T F O U R
The sale is run as a single confidential process with one team that has been on the
field since your first conversation. You are not handed off at the closing table to
people who are meeting you for the first time.

V P H A S E V · W E A L T H I N T E G R A T I O N
Manage the wealth.
The most important phase, and the point of all the others. Phase V is not the end
of the journey, it is the beginning of your next chapter. It is also where the hardest
statistic in this business gets decided: roughly three in four owners report
profound regret within a year of selling (widely cited EPI / industry research; [VERIFY/cite final source]), rarely about
the money. About what comes next.
“This is where Sandro's entire expertise comes together.”
Every dimension of post-sale financial life, managed with the same rigor the journey demanded, by the one team that has
been on the field since the first conversation, and never leaves it.
Define the next act, before you need it
For decades the business was your identity, calendar, and purpose, and the “golf-course retirement” loses its shine
within 6 to 18 months. Owners who land well decide where their energy goes next: a venture, a board seat,
philanthropy, family. You are not retiring
from a business; you are funding what comes next.
